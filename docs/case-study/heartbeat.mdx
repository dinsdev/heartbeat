## The question

A model that labels heartbeats is only useful on people it has not seen. So I kept patients apart, wrote down what I would report before looking, and ran the test once.

The model is a small 1D CNN reading one ECG lead from the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, next to a logistic-regression baseline that gets the same data. It trained on DS1, which is 22 patients. Its width (1) and epoch count (10) came from cross-validation inside that set, with patients kept apart (DS1 out-of-fold, MPS). DS2 stayed sealed until the end.

## The trap, shown

Split heartbeats at random and the same person ends up on both sides of the split. The model can then score well by recognizing the person. Here is the same CNN on the same 22 patients, split both ways. Both rows are DS1 out-of-fold, MPS, and neither touches DS2.

| Split | Macro F1 over N, S and V |
| --- | --- |
| Beats at random | 0.8176 (0.7478 to 0.8573) |
| Patients kept apart | 0.6583 (0.5680 to 0.7782) |

Accuracy on the random split was 0.9512 (0.9279 to 0.9727) (DS1 out-of-fold, MPS). That is the number papers often lead with, and it appears here and nowhere else on this page.

I did not find this first. de Chazal and colleagues (2004) showed it: their Table VI sets a beat-level random split against a split by record, inside DS1. I do not compare my numbers with theirs on this page. Theirs come from a two-lead classifier tested on all 22 DS2 records, and mine from one lead on the 21 patients left after the leak fix below.

## The test

## The CNN against the baseline

On 21 patients it never saw, the CNN and the logistic-regression baseline show no clear difference: the CNN's macro F1 over N, S and V minus the baseline's is -0.0307, with an interval from -0.1313 to 0.1202 that includes zero. The CNN still ships, as the plan said before the test.

Those are DS2 test numbers on CPU, and both models are scored on the same bootstrap draws of patients.

## The 201 and 202 leak

Records 201 and 202 come from the same person, and the standard split puts one on each side. Left alone, that hands the test a patient the model trained on. So record 202 is left out everywhere and the patient sits only in DS1.

## Limits

- Beat locations come from the expert annotations, so these numbers assume every beat has already been found. The demo does not find beats on its own.
- F is one patient on each side, so its numbers describe two people.
- Record 232 drives the S result. Without it, the CNN's S recall is 0.6400 (0.1683 to 0.8395) (DS2 test, CPU).
- Calibrated probabilities are fragile across patients, because calibration depends on how common each class is and that varies widely from person to person. The CNN's pooled calibration error is 0.0054 for S and 0.0525 for V (DS2 test, CPU), and a pooled figure says nothing about any one patient.
- A label shows one beat late, because the model reads the gap to the next beat.
- The model reads one lead and was tested on 21 patients (DS2 test, CPU).
- Q beats are not scored; DS2 has 7 of them (DS2 test, CPU).
- These numbers say nothing about how common arrhythmias are. PhysioNet notes that 25 of the recordings were chosen to include uncommon ones, so the database's prevalence is not a population's.
- This is a research demo, not medical advice. Don't use it for a health decision.

## How to check it

The pre-registered plan, the freeze record, the append-only test log, the leakage tests in CI and the parity tests are all in the repo. To check them:

```bash
uv run heartbeat verify
```

That takes seconds once the data is downloaded. `uv run heartbeat rebuild` reruns every stage from the raw files, retraining the models. I ran it from a fresh clone on the same Mac that trained the model. It retrained twice and stopped twice before it reached the reproduction, because that step needs a clean tree: I committed the rewritten result files each time and ran it again. The results matched a second fresh clone apart from run names, commits, paths and wall times, the model files, fixtures and cards matched byte for byte, and the reproduction matched the sealed report. I have not tried it on another machine. The times and the exact messages are in `docs/WORK_LEDGER.md`.
